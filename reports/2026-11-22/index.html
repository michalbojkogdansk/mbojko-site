Observability Pipelines Get a Gartner Quadrant as Cribl Buys Tero

Period November 9 - November 22, 2026
Author Michał Bojko
Type Bi-weekly Update

KubeCon North America in Salt Lake City, held November 9 to 12, set the date for most product news in the first week of the period, and Gartner's first Magic Quadrant for Observability Pipelines shaped the second. Cribl announced its third acquisition in about four months, Elastic and Honeycomb published growth figures, Dynatrace widened its work with Google Cloud, and Splunk released its November security advisories, including a 9.9-rated flaw in Splunk SOAR.

Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Pipelines

On November 17, three tracked vendors announced Leader placements in Gartner's inaugural Magic Quadrant for Observability Pipelines, dated November 12. The category covers products that collect, route, transform and reduce telemetry before it reaches a storage or analysis backend. Cribl said Cribl Stream was positioned highest in Ability to Execute and furthest in Completeness of Vision, and that half of the Fortune 100 rely on Cribl for IT and security data. Datadog said it was named a Leader, its second Leader placement in Gartner research this year. Dynatrace said Bindplane was named a Leader and, in the companion Critical Capabilities report, ranked highest in three of five use cases: DevOps/AppDev (3.73 out of 5), Platform Operations (3.70) and SecOps (3.68, tied), and placed in the top two in all five. Dynatrace announced the Bindplane acquisition in April and has kept it available as a standalone product for multi-vendor environments, as noted in the consolidation analysis. The placements and scores above come from the vendors' own releases. The full report is available from Gartner.

Cribl Buys Tero

On November 10, Cribl announced the acquisition of Tero, a startup whose technology measures how much of an organization's telemetry is low-value and where that data sits across observability tools. It is Cribl's third acquisition in about four months, after CardinalOps in July and Radiant Security's AI SOC technology in August, both of which feed the Cribl Detect SIEM covered in Update #9. Tero's co-founders Ben Johnson, Adam Cohen and Nihar Singhal join Cribl to lead its observability roadmap, covering host monitoring, application performance monitoring, federated observability and AI-native observability. Johnson previously co-founded Timber, the company behind the open-source Vector pipeline. Terms were not disclosed. With Tero and the Gartner placement in the same period, Cribl's announced roadmap now reaches from pipeline management into security analytics and application monitoring.

KubeCon Launches From Dash0 and New Relic

On November 10, Dash0 made continuous profiling generally available, 12 weeks after acquiring Polar Signals in August. An eBPF agent on each Kubernetes node profiles every process in every language without code changes, and profiles are stored in OpenTelemetry format next to traces, logs and metrics. Polar Signals' own measurements put overhead under 1% even under heavy CPU load. The Parca project remains open source, and pricing is $0.60 per million samples.

The same day, New Relic put its Autopilot Kubernetes Agent into public preview. The agent runs more than 90 diagnostic detectors across Kubernetes events, object descriptions, logs and APM traces, covering cases such as noisy neighbors, scheduling failures, network errors and ingress misconfigurations. It identifies a root cause and produces remediation code for an engineer to review, with configurable human-in-the-loop steps, and draws on New Relic's Ground Truth context engine.

Elastic and Honeycomb Report Growth

On November 19, Elastic reported fiscal second-quarter 2027 results for the quarter ended October 31. Revenue was $496 million, up 17% year over year, sales-led subscription revenue was $415 million, up 19%, and current remaining performance obligations reached $1.157 billion, up 19%. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.94. Third-quarter revenue guidance of $507.5 million to $508.5 million implies 12.9% growth, and full-year guidance is $2.000 billion to $2.008 billion, with non-GAAP EPS guidance raised to $3.35 to $3.41. The board authorized a new $500 million share repurchase program. SiliconANGLE reported that the third-quarter guidance came in below analyst estimates of $517.6 million and that the shares fell about 8% in extended trading.

On November 18, Honeycomb, which is private, published growth milestones. ARR grew more than 70% over four quarters, net revenue retention was 125% and the number of AI-native customers rose 130%. AI agents and MCP clients now run more than 4.5 million queries a month against Honeycomb, up from about 20,000 in January. Honeycomb did not disclose an absolute ARR figure. Chief Revenue Officer Matt Nelson was promoted to President.

Dynatrace and Google Cloud

On November 20, Dynatrace expanded its collaboration with Google Cloud. Dynatrace data, its Grail data lakehouse, causal AI and automation become available inside Gemini Enterprise agent workflows through natural-language queries, and AI observability for Gemini-enabled environments traces models, agents and applications with latency, token consumption and cost. A Dynatrace plugin is listed in Google's Antigravity Plugin Marketplace, and the Gemini Enterprise integrations are offered through Google Cloud Marketplace. The announcement was published as a blog post and gives no preview or general availability label.

Analyst Recognitions

Two further recognitions were announced on November 12. Coralogix said Gartner named it a Market Shaper in the Emerging Market Quadrant for AI FinOps Tools, Startup Vendors category, citing AI cost visibility across coding assistants, production applications and agent workflows. Riverbed said it was named a Leader in the 2026 GigaOm Radar for AIOps, positioned in the Agile Optimizers quadrant, and that its customers have run more than half a billion cumulative AI-driven automations. Both descriptions come from the vendors' releases.

Splunk Security Advisories

On November 18, Splunk published five advisories that, according to security press coverage, fix 146 vulnerabilities, following an advance notice the previous week. The most severe, CVE-2026-131253, is rated CVSS 9.9 and affects Splunk SOAR below 8.8.0: the App Wizard places a user-supplied name into connector code without validation, so a logged-in user with app and playbook rights can run arbitrary code. A second SOAR flaw, CVE-2026-131287, is rated 9.6. Both are fixed in SOAR 8.8.0, covered by SVD-2026-1108. Splunk Enterprise received fixes for two improper authentication flaws, CVE-2026-76536 (9.4) and CVE-2026-76535 (9.1), which coverage describes as reachable by remote attackers without a login. The fixed versions are 10.6.1, 10.4.5, 10.2.9, 10.0.12 and 9.4.17, which supersede the SVD-2026-1008 versions covered in Update #11. Splunk Enterprise Security 8.7.0 fixes CVE-2026-131202 (9.1). No exploitation has been reported.

What to Watch

AWS re:Invent runs from November 30 to December 4, where Honeycomb plans to demonstrate its AI SRE agents. Elastic's third quarter ends January 31 against guidance below analyst estimates. New Relic's Autopilot Kubernetes Agent remains in public preview, and Dynatrace has not given availability stages for the Gemini Enterprise integrations. Splunk administrators who upgraded for the October advisories need a further upgrade to the November fixed versions.
