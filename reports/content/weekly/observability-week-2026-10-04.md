## Section 3: Web Post

**What's Moving in Observability: September 28 to October 4, 2026**

The first week of Q4 opened with a completed acquisition and a new SIEM product that restructures how vendors can price detection capabilities. Both happened in Chicago.

### Dynatrace closes the Arize deal

Dynatrace completed its $915 million acquisition of Arize AI on October 1. The combination joins Dynatrace's runtime observability with Arize's tools for LLM tracing, evaluation, and experimentation: the open-source Phoenix library and the managed Arize AX product. The gap Dynatrace cited at announcement in August remains the framing at close. AI development tooling and operations tooling evolved as separate disciplines, and teams have had no mechanism to correlate agent behavior with system performance or business outcomes without this kind of integration. Arize tools remain available independently for teams not running Dynatrace.

The acquisition closed six weeks after announcement, faster than typical M&A timelines for deals of this size. Arize's team and tooling now operate as Dynatrace's AI observability business unit.

### Cribl launches a SIEM

At CriblCon 26 in Chicago (September 29-30), Cribl announced Cribl Detect, a SIEM platform built on its existing data pipeline infrastructure. The architectural distinction Cribl is drawing: Detect runs detections across data wherever it resides rather than requiring centralization into a log store first. Pricing is infrastructure-based, not data-ingest-based. Cribl claims up to 50% cost reduction compared to competing SIEMs on this model.

Detect ships with AI SOC triage, detection posture management, and SOAR capabilities. The product draws on two acquisitions from earlier this year: CardinalOps (detection engineering tooling, July 2026) and AI SOC assets from Radiant Security (August 2026). Cribl reported surpassing $300 million ARR and 1,500 customers at the event.

CriblCon also produced StreamAI, a gateway that routes model requests by cost and performance, redacts sensitive data from prompts and responses before they reach model providers, and logs all interactions for audit purposes. StreamAI is live for Cribl.Cloud customers now.

The Cribl launches extend a competitive dynamic visible for most of Q3: vendors with data pipeline foundations are moving into security analytics, while SIEM vendors are adding data fabric and pipeline capabilities. Cribl is arriving from the pipeline side. Splunk's .conf26 announcements three weeks ago arrived from the SIEM side.

### Leadership and product updates elsewhere

New Relic appointed Nandini Ramani as CEO and board member effective October 1. Ramani previously led observability, search, and compliance at AWS, and has held executive roles at Oracle and X. She replaces Ashan Willy, who moves to advisory capacity. New Relic Now, a product event scheduled for October 6, is expected to carry AI observability announcements under her tenure.

Honeycomb released AI Ecosystem in early access on September 29, adding fleet-level analysis for teams monitoring multi-agent AI systems. Canvas Connectors, shipping alongside it, allow the Canvas AI agent to pull context from code repositories, incident histories, and runbooks before generating resolutions. The release follows Agent Timeline (GA in June) and moves Honeycomb from individual agent tracing to fleet-scale analysis.

Grafana Labs shipped v13.3.0 GA on September 30. Key items: the Text visualization panel was rebuilt to fix long-standing Markdown and image rendering limitations; Recommended Usage Alerts (GA) provides baseline-based cost warnings without custom configuration; Loki 4.0 ships with significant query performance improvements.
