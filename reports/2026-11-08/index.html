Earnings, a Forrester Wave and Agents Built Into the Developer Workflow

Period October 26 - November 8, 2026
Author Michał Bojko
Type Bi-weekly Update

Earnings season and an analyst report set the frame for the last week of October and the first week of November. Datadog and Dynatrace reported quarterly results days apart, and both were named Leaders in the same Forrester Wave for AIOps platforms. Honeycomb, New Relic and Datadog each moved agents closer to the developer's daily workflow, Dynatrace and CrowdStrike announced a security partnership, and Splunk added a second round of critical Enterprise fixes.

Quarterly Results

On November 4, Datadog reported third-quarter revenue of $1.20 billion, up 35% year over year. About 4,860 customers had ARR of $100,000 or more, up from about 4,060 a year earlier. Non-GAAP operating income was $334 million, a 28% margin, non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.81, and free cash flow was $251 million. Datadog guided fourth-quarter revenue to $1.19 billion to $1.20 billion and full-year 2026 revenue to $4.515 billion to $4.525 billion. The release also introduced Scout 1.0, the first specialized model from Datadog's AI Research team, which now powers Bits Investigation.

A day earlier, on November 3, Dynatrace reported fiscal second-quarter 2027 results, for the quarter ended September 30, the day before the Arize acquisition covered in Update #9 closed. ARR reached $2,207 million and total revenue $580 million, up 16% and 17% respectively, both as reported and in constant currency. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.54, compared with $0.44 a year earlier. Full-year ARR guidance rose to $2,393 million to $2,413 million and revenue guidance to $2,330 million to $2,340 million, while non-GAAP EPS guidance moved down to $1.88 to $1.90 from $1.97 to $1.99. The board authorized an additional $1 billion share repurchase, leaving about $1.3 billion of capacity. Dynatrace said more than 1,100 customers use its agents for autonomous operations, up from more than 800 the previous quarter.

Forrester Wave: AIOps Platforms, Q4 2026

Forrester evaluated 10 providers against 24 criteria in its AIOps Platforms report for Q4 2026, and two tracked vendors announced Leader placements within a day of each other. On October 29, Datadog said it received the highest possible score in eleven criteria, including Innovation, Roadmap, and ModelOps and AI Governance, and said Bits AI has passed 10,000 paying customers. On October 30, Dynatrace said it received the highest score of all evaluated vendors in the Current Offering category and the highest possible score in 16 criteria, including causal root cause analysis, autonomous AI SRE and orchestration, and algorithmic trust and explainability. Both summaries come from the vendors' own releases. The full report is available from Forrester.

Agents Move Into the Developer Workflow

On November 3, Honeycomb introduced AI SRE agents in early access. A Personal Agent works with an individual engineer, using the team's runbooks and systems, keeping chats private, showing how it reached a conclusion and acting only when told to. Team Agents run in the background on a schedule, a trigger or an SLO toward goals such as watching a release, improving instrumentation or investigating alerts. Both build a shared memory in which each finding links to the query, trace, deploy or ticket behind it and is labeled as confirmed or as a hypothesis. Within the permissions a team grants, agents can file tickets, page responders and draft fixes. Honeycomb cites a forthcoming survey of its own in which 76% of respondents said AI-generated code has grown faster than their team's ability to review and monitor it.

On October 28, New Relic brought Change Tracking into GitHub Copilot in public preview. It runs checks against a baseline before merge, canary gates after deployment and regression detection, and sends regressions to GitHub Issues and Copilot for fixes, with updates to Slack. Two related integrations are generally available: an open-source Preflight integration that shows model efficiency, latency and token consumption of coding agents in the IDE and CLI, and an Autopilot integration for GitHub MCP Server that gives New Relic's SRE agent codebase context when triaging alerts. New Relic cites its 2026 State of AI Coding report, in which 94% of leaders rated AI-generated code as higher quality at review time and 78% reported more incidents after deployment. A day later, New Relic appointed Steve Lucas, chairman and CEO of Boomi and former CEO of iCIMS and Marketo, as Chair of its Board of Directors.

Dynatrace and CrowdStrike Partner on Security Data

At Fal.Con Europe in Barcelona on November 2, CrowdStrike and Dynatrace announced a partnership positioned against legacy SIEM. Three integrations are available now: federated search from the Falcon platform across Dynatrace observability and log data without copying it, incident response that combines CrowdStrike endpoint telemetry with Dynatrace log, application and infrastructure data, and log and telemetry routing between the two platforms through Bindplane by Dynatrace. No pricing was given. The announcement follows Cribl's entry into SIEM covered in Update #9, with observability data again being offered as input for security operations.

Product Updates From Grafana and Dash0

Grafana Labs released Grafana 13.3 in late October, with the release post on October 29. Cross-dashboard variables, defined once and reused across dashboards in a folder or globally, entered public preview in all editions, as did Notebooks, which combine panels, Explore queries, Assistant findings and notes in one shareable document. The release also starts a visual redesign in public preview and lets Grafana Assistant propose a dashboard plan for review before building it, and Assistant edits can now be undone.

On October 29, Dash0 added Session Replay that stores browser recordings as log records through the same OpenTelemetry endpoint, token, dataset and region as other telemetry. Each recording shares a trace ID with backend traces, recordings are kept for 30 days, and input values are masked in the browser by default. Cross-origin iframes such as payment forms are not recorded. The same day, Dash0 added CloudWatch metrics scraping every five minutes through a read-only IAM role, with tag and region filters applied before metrics are requested, while Metric Streams remain the option for alerts that need data within seconds.

Splunk Security Advisories

Splunk issued two further Enterprise advisories after the ones covered in Update #10. SVD-2026-1008, published on October 21 and missed by the previous period's collection, groups internally found issues into four CVEs, two of them rated CVSS 9.9: CVE-2026-76299 (improper access control) and CVE-2026-76302 (improper neutralization). Fixed versions are 10.4.4, 10.2.8, 10.0.11 and 9.4.16, which supersede the versions recommended for CVE-2026-76268. The advisory gives no detail on the individual flaws and reports no exploitation. On October 28, SVD-2026-1015 addressed high-severity Node.js vulnerabilities in several Splunk products: Splunk Enterprise 10.0 is affected with a fix planned, 9.4 and 10.2 or later are not, and remediation for other products is in progress.

What to Watch

Honeycomb plans to demonstrate its AI SRE agents at AWS re:Invent from November 30 to December 4. Datadog has not given a general availability date for Scout 1.0, and several Grafana 13.3 features remain in public preview. Splunk Enterprise administrators who patched for the October advisories need a further upgrade to the SVD-2026-1008 fixed versions.
